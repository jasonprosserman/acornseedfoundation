What they do

Community Justice Collective is a movement lawyering organization. They provide legal support to the organizers, tenant groups, and grassroots campaigns that larger legal clinics cannot take on. They do it as a partner in the work, not a service provider at arm's length.

  • Legal advice and representation for organizers and movements
  • Know-your-rights education
  • Support for tenant and community campaigns
What sets them apart

The people CJC defends are the ones institutions with money or power try to slow down through the legal process: tenant unions, encampment residents, mutual-aid groups, land defenders. They are a small team working from a donated office, and by their own account they have never lost a client they started with. They report having beaten dozens of eviction and encampment charges. CJC decides for themselves whom they represent and how, and that independence is exactly why it works.

What they’ve accomplished

According to their annual reports, CJC has acted as counsel for rent strikes across the city. One precedent-setting win at 1440–1442 Lawrence Avenue West reversed an illegal eviction and compelled the landlord to fix the building. They report having influenced provincial bail-reform legislation. They have also partnered with U of T's Faculty of Law on a legal-tech tool for marginalized communities, mentored 13 aspiring lawyers, and launched Counsel Call, which became Canada's top "How To" podcast. They used part of our grant to pay tenant leaders to become organizers, and are now expanding into workers' rights.
