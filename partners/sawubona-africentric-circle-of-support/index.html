What they do

Black families in the GTA raising children and adults with disabilities and special needs

Sawubona is a Zulu greeting that means "I see you." The organization was founded by parents who found that disability services were not built with Black families in mind, and who wanted a place where families like theirs could be seen. It runs peer support circles, one-to-one navigation support, workshops, and community gatherings, and equips caregivers to advocate for their family members inside systems that are often hard to move.

  • Peer support circles for caregivers
  • One-to-one help navigating services and funding
  • Workshops and a caregiver toolkit in English and French
  • Community events that bring isolated families together
  • Advocacy within the disability and education systems
Why we fund them

Sawubona works at the intersection of two communities that each face barriers on their own, and it was built by the people living that experience. It has delivered steadily for years on project funding and has a five-year plan for what comes next. Our advisory council chose Sawubona because the need is clear, the leadership is trusted by the families it serves, and multi-year funding lets a caregiver-led organization stop fundraising long enough to grow.
